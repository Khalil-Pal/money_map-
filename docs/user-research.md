# User research: student spending survey

**When:** Oct 14, 2026 (Google Form, in Arabic) · **Who:** 7 students · **Summarized:** Nov 11, 2026

A small, informal sample (people Khalil knows, all answers within about two hours). It points in a
direction; it doesn't prove anything. Answers are anonymous; translations are by meaning.

## Questions and answers

**1. How do you track your spending?** (كيف بتتعقب إنفاقك المالي)

| Answer | Count |
|---|---|
| Notes app on the phone | 5 |
| I don't track it | 2 |

**2. Do you usually run out of money before the month ends, or before your salary or allowance
arrives?** (هل بالعادة بتفلس قبل نهاية الشهر او قبل ما يوصلك راتبك/مصروفك من اهلك؟)

| Answer | Count |
|---|---|
| Always | 1 |
| Usually | 3 |
| Sometimes | 2 |
| Rarely | 1 |

6 of 7 run out at least sometimes; 4 of 7 usually or always.

**3. Why do you run out before the month ends?** (ما هو سبب افلاسك قبل نهاية الشهر؟)

All 7 chose "rising prices". When every answer is the same option, the form's order may have
influenced it, so this result is weak.

**4. Have you tried a spending app before?** (هل قمت قبل هالمرة بتجربة تطبيقات لتنظيم الانفاق المالي؟)

No: 7 of 7.

**5. What would make you use a spending app weekly or monthly?** (open answer)

- "If it helps me save money, shows me exactly how I spend, and makes a difference at the end of
  the month, I'll definitely use it."
- "If it worked for others."
- "The quality of the app."
- "Earning money."
- Skeptic: "A spending app doesn't work, because unexpected things happen."
- Unclear: something like "when my spending goes wrong with nothing real to show for it".
- One joke answer.

## What we take from it

1. **The competitor is the phone's notes app**, not another budgeting app. Entering a payment must
   be about as fast as typing a note → the quick-add bar (`250 food lunch` + Enter) is core, not an
   extra.
2. **The pain is running out before the next money arrives.** The dashboard's headline should be
   "left to spend · days to go · per day", not only a chart.
3. **Plans meet surprises.** Changing a limit mid-month must be quick, and going over a limit
   should inform, not scold.
4. **Nobody has used such an app**, so first use must need no explanation.
5. **Trust comes from quality and from others using it** → a polished design (planned after Dec 1)
   and a few real users from this group.

## Open risk

People track on their phones; Jeib is a desktop app (mobile is out of scope, see `CLAUDE.md`).
Payments will be entered later at the computer, not at the till. Mitigation ideas: very fast
entry, and later a "paste several lines from your notes" import that reuses the quick-add parser.
Re-check with the Dec 1 test users whether this stops them using it.
